### Building Adaptability in the Age of AI

Technology doesn't wait for you to feel ready. New tools — especially AI-powered ones — show up constantly, and the people who thrive are the ones who can learn them quickly instead of avoiding them. This assignment puts you in that exact situation on purpose: you'll pick an unfamiliar tool, give yourself only 48 hours to get functional with it, and pay close attention to *how* you learn, not just *what* you learn.

---

## Learning Objective

By the end of this assignment, you will be able to:
- Learn a new, unfamiliar tool under a real time constraint
- Identify the specific strategies that help you adapt quickly
- Reflect honestly on the discomfort of not knowing something yet, and how you moved through it

---

## Step 1 — Choose Your Tool (Do this first, before the clock starts)

Select a tool or app you have **never used before**. It should be:

- **New to you** — not something you've dabbled in before
- **Ideally AI-related** (an AI writing tool, image generator, AI-powered productivity app, coding assistant, transcription tool, etc.) — though a non-AI tool is acceptable if you check with your instructor first
- **Free or accessible** without a paid subscription
- **Realistic in scope** — something you could plausibly learn the basics of in two days, not a professional-grade software suite that takes months to master

**Need ideas?** Ask your instructor for a list of suggested tools, or propose your own for approval before starting the clock.

## Step 3 — Learn the Tool (Over the Next 48 Hours)

Dive in. Explore the tool, watch tutorials if needed, experiment, make mistakes, and figure it out. There's no single "right" way to do this — the point is to notice your *own* process as you go.

### Journal Requirement

Keep a short journal during the 48 hours. You don't need to write an essay each time — a few honest sentences per entry is enough. Make **at least 3 journal entries** across the two days (for example: one shortly after you start, one partway through, and one near the end).

For each entry, note:
- **What you tried** (what did you do in this session?)
- **What was confusing or frustrating** (where did you get stuck? What made you want to give up?)
- **What helped** (a tutorial, a friend, trial and error, reading the FAQ, something else?)
- **How you felt** (be honest — overwhelmed, curious, bored, excited, embarrassed, etc.)

## Step 5 — Final Reflection (Write this after your 48 hours are up)

Write a reflection of **8–12 sentences** responding to the following:

- What made this tool difficult to learn at first? Be specific.
- Which strategy helped you adapt the *fastest* — and why do you think it worked?
- Did your feelings about the challenge change from the beginning to the end? How?
- What does this experience tell you about how you personally learn new things under pressure?
- How might this same approach help you the next time a new tool, app, or AI system shows up unexpectedly at school or work?
