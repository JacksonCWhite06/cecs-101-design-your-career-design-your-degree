# Career 1: Information Security Analyst

## Source(s) used

- O*NET
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook
- LinkedIn

## What people in this career actually do day-to-day

Information security analysts monitor and protect an organization's computer systems, networks, and information. Their work can include identifying vulnerabilities, monitoring for suspicious activity, responding to security incidents, recommending security improvements, and making sure security controls are properly implemented. They may also help train employees on security practices.

## Work environment

Information security analysts usually work in office or technology-focused environments and spend much of their time working on computers. Some positions can be remote or hybrid. They frequently work with other IT employees, management, and users throughout an organization. During security incidents, they may sometimes need to work outside normal business hours.

## Required education and training

A bachelor's degree in cybersecurity, computer science, information technology, or a related field is commonly required. Employers may also prefer professional certifications such as CompTIA Security+, CySA+, CISSP, or other security certifications. Previous IT or networking experience can also be valuable.

## Key skills and abilities required

1. Reading comprehension
2. Critical thinking
3. Active listening
4. Speaking
5. Writing

# Career 2: AI / Machine Learning Specialist

## Source(s) used

- O*NET
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook

## What people in this career actually do day-to-day

AI and machine learning professionals work with large amounts of data and create models that can recognize patterns, make predictions, or automate tasks. Their daily work can include preparing data, writing Python code, testing machine learning models, evaluating their accuracy, improving algorithms, and explaining results to other employees or decision makers.

Because BLS and O*NET do not have one single category called "AI/ML Specialist," the closely related Data Scientist occupation was used for salary and employment information.

## Work environment

Most AI and machine learning professionals work in office, research, technology, or remote environments. The work is mostly computer-based and usually involves working with software engineers, data scientists, researchers, analysts, and business teams.

## Required education and training

A bachelor's degree in computer science, artificial intelligence, data science, mathematics, or a related field can qualify someone for many positions. However, some employers prefer a master's degree, especially for more advanced machine learning or research positions. Strong programming, mathematics, statistics, and machine learning knowledge are important.

## Key skills and abilities required

2. Critical thinking
3. Reading comprehension
4. Active learning
5. Speaking

# Career 3: Computer Network Architect / Cybersecurity Architect

## Source(s) used

- O*NET
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook
- LinkedIn

## What people in this career actually do day-to-day

Computer network architects design and improve the networks organizations rely on. They determine how networks should be structured, select hardware and software, evaluate security requirements, test systems, troubleshoot problems, and plan future upgrades. A cybersecurity-focused architect would place additional emphasis on designing networks and systems so that security is built into them from the beginning.

## Work environment

Network architects usually work in office or technology environments and spend most of their time working on computers. They collaborate with system administrators, cybersecurity teams, managers, engineers, vendors, and other technology professionals. They may occasionally work in server rooms or data centers.

## Required education and training

A bachelor's degree in cybersecurity, information technology, computer science, engineering, or a related area is typically required. Unlike many entry-level analyst positions, network architect positions also commonly require several years of previous IT, networking, or systems experience.

## Key skills and abilities required

1. Critical thinking
2. Reading comprehension
3. Active listening
4. Writing
5. Speaking

## Salary range

- Median: $134,050
- Experienced: $202,680+

## Job outlook

Projected growth rate: 8% from 2025–2035.

## Industries and employers that hire for this role

1. Computer systems design companies
2. Financial and insurance companies
3. Telecommunications companies
4. Large corporations
5. Government and defense organizations

## Connection to my Module 2 findings

This career connects with several of the themes I identified in Module 2, including technical problem solving, cybersecurity, learning how systems work, adaptability, and continuous learning. It also gives me a possible long-term career path after gaining experience as a cybersecurity analyst.

## What excites me about this career

I like that this career involves looking at cybersecurity from a larger perspective instead of focusing only on one incident or individual system. An architect can help determine how an organization's entire technology environment should be designed and protected.

## What concerns me or gives me pause

## My T-Shape for this career

Deep expertise: Secure network and system architecture.

Adjacent skills: Cybersecurity operations, cloud security, networking, artificial intelligence, programming, risk management, communication, and project management.
