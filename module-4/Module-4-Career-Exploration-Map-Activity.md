# Career Exploration Map Activity

This activity asks you to research at least three careers in depth using professional tools. You will build a Career Exploration Map — a structured, comparative overview of the careers you find most compelling. This becomes a reference document you will use in Section 4.5 when building your Career Plan.

---

Based on your RIASEC profile, your self-assessment, and your Ideation Intentions, identify three careers you want to explore in depth.

- One can be a career that surprised you in your RIASEC results.

**What people in this career actually do day-to-day:**
*(Summarize the core tasks and responsibilities — in your own words.)*
Information security analysts monitor and protect an organization's computer systems, networks, and information. Their work can include identifying vulnerabilities, monitoring for suspicious activity, responding to security incidents, recommending security improvements, and making sure security controls are properly implemented. They may also help train employees on security practices.
**Work environment:**
*(Where do they work? Office? Field? Hospital? Remote? With people? Alone? Physical? Sedentary?)*
Information security analysts usually work in office or technology-focused environments and spend much of their time working on computers. Some positions can be remote or hybrid. They frequently work with other IT employees, management, and users throughout an organization. During security incidents, they may sometimes need to work outside normal business hours.
**Required education and training:**
*(What degree level is typically required? Any certifications, licenses, or graduate degrees needed?)*
A bachelor's degree in cybersecurity, computer science, information technology, or a related field is commonly required. Employers may also prefer professional certifications such as CompTIA Security+, CySA+, CISSP, or other security certifications. Previous IT or networking experience can also be valuable.

## Career 3: [Computer Network Architect / Cybersecurity Architect]

**Source(s) used:**
- [ ] O*NET

- [ ] Other

**What people in this career actually do day-to-day:**
*(Summarize the core tasks and responsibilities — in your own words.)*
Computer network architects design and improve the networks organizations rely on. They determine how networks should be structured, select hardware and software, evaluate security requirements, test systems, troubleshoot problems, and plan future upgrades. A cybersecurity-focused architect would place additional emphasis on designing networks and systems so that security is built into them from the beginning.
**Work environment:**
*(Where do they work? Office? Field? Hospital? Remote? With people? Alone? Physical? Sedentary?)*
Network architects usually work in office or technology environments and spend most of their time working on computers. They collaborate with system administrators, cybersecurity teams, managers, engineers, vendors, and other technology professionals. They may occasionally work in server rooms or data centers.
**Required education and training:**
*(What degree level is typically required? Any certifications, licenses, or graduate degrees needed?)*
A bachelor's degree in cybersecurity, information technology, computer science, engineering, or a related area is typically required. Unlike many entry-level analyst positions, network architect positions also commonly require several years of previous IT, networking, or systems experience.
